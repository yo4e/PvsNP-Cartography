# A concrete compiler for the BV14 query tree

**Status.** Written construction and soundness derivation for a specified verifier variant. The proof uses the published ACSP reduction and bivariate agreement lemma as external results. No complete verifier implementation or independent formal verification is attached. The choice of address layout, root embedding, leaf indexing and public-seed constraints is part of this variant.

## The oracle and its addresses

Put the external algebraic word over a characteristic-two field F of size 2^k. For the proximity recursion fix a constant rate exponent a≥3. Write h=0 if k≤2a, and h=1+floor(log₂(k−2a)) otherwise. At a node of dimension d>2a, set l=floor(d/2). The row child has dimension l+a, the column child d−l. The excess above 2a decreases at least as fast as repeated halving. Represent an internal oracle cell by tree ID, path shape, edge labels, its point within the node, and bit-of-symbol index. Use two header bits per level (row, column, or unused), with unused suffix positions. Omit unused edges from the payload and zero-pad the payload at its end. This is the layout exercised by verify_recursive.py. Shape determines the widths of all labels. Edge width plus child dimension is d+a for a row and d+a−1 for a column. Thus the entire edge-and-point payload has at most k+ah bits. Decoding is injective on valid cells.

The two RS trees have different tree IDs. The root of each refers to the corresponding actual algebraic word, including when a leaf query redirects to it. A column point refers to the same row point after swapping coordinates as below. Overlap between a row and its parent always refers to the parent. Only the other extension cells have independent physical storage. Hence every assignment to the physical bits supplies the single consistent partial bivariate function used at each parent in the soundness argument. In particular, two names for a shared point cannot supply different values. Invalid padded addresses may take any value.

## Routing a fixed leaf query

For a fixed down tape, write the current word as (U,V), of lengths l and d−l. All fresh choices per level (shape and at most a padding bits) are fixed.

- A row step saves V as edge label and descends with (U,A), where A consists of a fresh bits.
- A column step saves (U,A') and descends with V, where A' consists of a−1 fresh bits.
- At a leaf of dimension b≤2a, one may enumerate its 2^b **absolute** coordinates w. Alternatively enumerate the 2^b offsets w and set its query to the inherited leaf word XOR w. The latter order depends on the seed. The public decision circuit reorders the resulting answers to the original leaf coordinates, using that seed and the fixed tape.
- When climbing a row edge, the returned point has coordinates (U',v,t), with the last a bits split into a−1 auxiliary bits v and one selector t. Its field element is β+u'+aux(v)+t(c₀+β). Equation (9) places the parent at β+L₀, so return to it exactly when v=0 and t=0 for this basis. The returned parent coordinates are (U',V). Otherwise stop at that row. The printed last-bit test in BV14 §6.3 uses the opposite polarity from its displayed origin. For example, u'=v=0 gives β at t=0 and c₀ at t=1.
- When climbing a column edge, the returned column point V' supplies the row edge label. The stored column label (U,A') supplies the within-row coordinates (U,A',1), encoding c₀+u+aux(A'). This belongs to the auxiliary rectangle, disjoint from the parent copy. Stop at this row sibling.

On a fixed down path the root's k coordinate tokens each occur once, either in an edge label or the live word. Every successful row return restores the parent's downward coordinate ordering with only the leaf replacement still applied on surviving slots. In particular the row guard positions correspond to that row's fresh A; their values are constants, possibly modified by the fixed leaf slot. Column redirection retains both its label and live tokens and adds a constant. Hence the final path *shape* and stop depth are fixed for the entire slot, even as the labels vary with the root seed. Absolute-leaf indexing erases exactly u original tokens occurring in the replaced leaf word, with u≤2a. Under offset indexing each original token survives exactly once, possibly complemented. This gives respective ranks k−u and k of the final encoded map, and maximum fibers of size 2^u and 1 on a k-bit seed. If the final address is a root, the surviving token order is that root's field coordinate order. This argument depends on including the path labels until their data are restored at a return.

## Field and word alignment

BV14 §4.4 calls for testing words F→F. Its §6 presentation uses an affine domain with space for a shift and extra independent basis elements. Work over an extension E with F a subfield, on the affine domain θ+F. Interpret the virtual root symbol at θ+x as the embedded external word v(x). External root bits are shared with the ACSP. In an F-linear basis starting with 1, the other extension coordinates of the root value are constant zero, compiled as constants in the decision circuit. Internal oracle symbols are free E values.

For k≥a+1 a quadratic extension suffices. For example find a binary basis element c with absolute trace one and use E=F[θ]/(θ²+θ+c). The trace is a nonzero linear functional, and the image of x²+x is its trace-zero kernel. The root shift, the k binary basis vectors of F and a additional vectors can be chosen independent in E. At a row child β lies outside the low directions and the auxiliary coset has an independent shift. At a column child the subspace polynomial q has kernel L₀, so the images of the complementary basis and shift remain independent. At each subsequent node extend a basis by binary elimination. These operations take polynomial time in k for a given path, including forming subspace polynomials by the recurrence q'(X)=q(X)²+q(b)q(X) on adding direction b to a span. For the finitely many k below a fixed size threshold, directly check the constant-sized root table. One may enumerate its points as z XOR v over all offsets v, recovering the absolute order from the public z.

Distances for the two root alphabets agree exactly. An F-polynomial P provides the E-polynomial P(X−θ) of the same degree. Conversely take any E-polynomial Q and apply a fixed F-linear coefficient projection λ:E→F, λ restricted to F the identity, to Q(θ+X). At each x in F the resulting polynomial evaluates to λ(Q(θ+x)). Every agreement of Q with v survives, and the degree does not increase. Taking minima over the two codes proves the equality of symbol distances at any degree cutoff.

## Rejection at a single recursion node

The dependence on the proximity radius matters when it shrinks with k. Here is an explicit derivation using Ben-Sasson–Sudan, SIAM J. Comput. 38 (2008), Lemma 6.13. Let s=2^l, B=q(L₁) of size D=2^(d−l), and A=L'₀ of size 2^(a−1)s. The common rectangle is A×B. Every full row has size 2^a s, and the parent part in that row has s points. The row and column degree limits, inclusive, are s−1 and D/2^a−1. Write e_R,e_C for mean distances of full rows and columns to their respective codes. Restricting the rows to their half-domain A gives mean error at most 2e_R. The degree limits satisfy (s−1)≤|A|/4 and (D/2^a−1)≤|B|/8. The published agreement lemma gives a bivariate Q within c₀(2e_R+e_C) of the common rectangle.

For each row choose a closest polynomial on the full row. Its restriction to A and the corresponding row of Q, when unequal as polynomials, differ on at least 3/4 of A. Thus the fraction of such bad rows is at most (4/3)(2e_R+c₀(2e_R+e_C)). On good rows the total parent error contributed is at most 2^a e_R, since the parent part is a 2^(−a) fraction of each full row. Substitution P(X)=Q(X,q(X)) has degree at most (s−1)+s(D/2^a−1)=sD/2^a−1. The parent word's distance to the code therefore is at most K_a(e_R+e_C), where K_a=2^a+3(1+c₀).

The original down verifier selects a uniform row or column with equal branch weights and an independent uniform child seed. This follows by the explicit seed partitions. At a leaf the exact degree check has rejection at least the leaf's distance. Induction bounds root distance by (2K_a)^h times rejection. The multiplicative loss is polynomial in k for fixed a. Completeness follows from the polynomial decomposition in BV14 §6.1, eq. (8), and the row/column restrictions of its evaluation, with redirects implementing the same field points.

## Integrating the two roots and the clause verifier

In BV14 Theorem 4.2 write the external field size as 2^(l₀+s₀) (here k=l₀+s₀) and use a constant s₀≥c+12, with c≥0 the universal degree exponent offset. Allow strict degree cutoffs 2^(l₀+1) and 2^(l₀+c+1) to include the inclusive bounds on honest A,B in Theorem 4.2. These are the fixed rate exponents a_A=s₀−1 and a_B=s₀−c−1. Construct both recursions as above. On a NO instance the univariate constraint polynomial obtained from any such pair is nonzero. In equations (4)–(5) the selectors and vanishing polynomial have degree at most |H|=2^(l₀+1), clause factors contribute at most three times the A-degree, and the quotient term has the B-degree plus |H|. Its total degree is below |F|/4 for this slack, so it fails at least 3/4 of field points.

Let t be the number of field-word reads in the ACSP test (count repetitions). They are translations of the same uniform field point, including direct reads. Put ρ=1/(4t). If both external words have distance at most ρ from their low-degree codes, the evaluations agree with the closest polynomials at all those reads except with probability at most tρ. Thus the ACSP rejects with probability at least 1/2. Otherwise the far word's RS check rejects with probability at least ρ/(2K_a)^h. The minimum of these explicit budgets for the two roots gives an inverse-polynomial rejection bound γ. This step uses the equivalence of the NO instances and ACSP unsatisfiability in BV14 Theorem 4.2 and the construction of its selector polynomial. We need only inverse-polynomial rejection. The optional soundness boost in §4.4 composes existing slot maps with fixed seed translations and can be omitted here.

For uniform public z of k bits, enumerate all settings of the independent fresh tapes for each RS checker and evaluate all its leaf elements for each tape. There are polynomially many tapes because each level costs at most a+1 bits. AND the predicates with the ACSP predicate. Perfect completeness holds for every z and tape. For any one proof, the probability over z that some tape rejects dominates the original rejection probability over (z,tape). This enumerates *actual paths for each z* and never forces guesses for a control read from z. The field arithmetic and leaf checks may depend on z and its path labels. This is a polynomial-time public decision circuit because there are only polynomially many slots, each path has logarithmic depth and leaf size is constant.

To obtain fixed signed Boolean clauses, encode the gate computation with standard gate-equivalence clauses of width at most three, an output-true clause and proof cells (gate j,z). Proof answer inputs refer to their canonical query cells. Use cells b_i(z) for public input bits. Add two fixed unit clauses for each i, checking b_i(clamp_i(z,ε))=ε for ε=0,1, where clamp replaces that coordinate by the constant. Each pin read has rank k−1. For any arbitrary global assignment the sum of the two pin failure probabilities equals twice Pr_z[b_i(z)≠z_i]. For every seed rejected by the public decision circuit on the actual proof reads, at least one ordinary gate/output clause fails or a seed bit is wrong. A union bound shows that the sum of failure probabilities of the ordinary clauses plus half the corresponding pin sum is at least γ. Uniform selection from all M clauses thus has rejection probability at least γ/M. All honest gate values and seed pins simultaneously exist. One- or two-literal clauses can be padded by repeating literals. Constant circuit inputs are hardwired or fixed by a gate unit clause.

With separate polynomially many tags for gates, regions and symbol-bit indices the final width m=k+O(log k) for the target T=2^n family (k=n+O(log n) by BV14 Theorem 2.2 and §5.2, since l₀ is the sum of log T and O(log log T + log n); all arithmetic and gate descriptions have size poly(k)). Every occurrence has rank ≥k−1 for the offset variant, whence a fixed-input uniform proof-bit error η causes at most 3·2^(m−k+1)η expected failed tests on one global sampled proof polynomial. With normalized uniform clause weights, the union bound charges the error probabilities of at most three actual addresses. It applies to one fixed proof approximation and, by averaging, to an input-independent distribution over global polynomials of the stated degree. The same sample is evaluated at all addresses. This sufficient budget applies whenever it is strictly less than γ/M. All resulting read maps use only individual seed literals or constants, so replacing proof bits by a degree-d Boolean polynomial gives signed clause polynomials of degree at most 3d. This bound requires independently establishing the specified small error **on each needed fixed-input slice**. No complexity separation follows from the address analysis.
