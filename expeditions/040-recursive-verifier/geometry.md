# A translated root with one recursive level

Work over F=GF(2⁷) with polynomial-basis modulus t⁷+t+1 (0x83). An element of E=F[θ]/(θ²+θ+1) is stored as the 14-bit integer a+128b, meaning a+bθ. The quadratic has no root in F: for nonzero x other than one, x²+x+1=0 would give an element of order three in a group of order 127. Both roots thus lie in the quadratic extension. The physical root cells store one F-symbol apiece, and their high seven coordinates in E are zero. Physical extension cells store arbitrary E-symbols.

The root domain is θ+F. Choose the shift b₀=θ, the seven polynomial-basis elements of F as b₁,…,b₇, and (c₀,c₁,c₂)=(2θ,4θ,8θ), with integers standing for polynomial-basis bit strings. These eleven vectors are binary independent because the low and high halves of the extension basis are independent. Write U={0,…,7} for the GF(2)-linear span of 1,t,t² and let q(X)=∏_{u∈U}(X+u). Its ascending coefficient list in this basis is (0,98,24,0,123,0,0,0,1). It is linearized, with exactly U as its kernel. For r=0,…,15 put βᵣ=θ+(r shifted left by three coefficient positions) and yᵣ=q(βᵣ). The sixteen yᵣ are distinct. This is the k=7, a=3 case of BV14 §6.1, eqs. (6)–(9), Proposition 6.1 and Definition 6.2. [Primary paper, pp. 17–20](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2014/017/download/)

## Which points are shared

Write row coordinate w=u+8e₁+16e₂+32m as an integer decomposition, with u∈U. Its field abscissa is

`x(r,w)=βᵣ+u+e₁c₁+e₂c₂+m(c₀+βᵣ).`

Its bivariate point is (x(r,w),yᵣ). The unique physical word address for it is

`addr(r,w) = 128·floor(w/8) + 8r + (w mod 8).`

For w<8, x=βᵣ+u lies in the parent at translated root coordinate 8r+u. For w≥8, the point belongs to the extension for that row. The common rectangle uses w=32+c with 0≤c<32. Its abscissas are the same in every row because m=1 cancels βᵣ. The v-th point of column c is (x(v,32+c),yᵥ); the read uses addr(v,32+c). The other row-extension positions, w=8,…,31, participate in row tests and do not appear in a column test. There are 128 actual parent symbols, 896 free extension symbols, and 512 names shared between columns and rows. The 16 rows of 64 points and 32 columns of 16 points are all leaf tests, with respective strict degree cutoffs 8 and 2.

For z=u+8v, a fixed row fresh word f∈[0,8) and offset j∈[0,64) query row v at coordinate (u+8f) XOR j. A column lane with f∈[0,4) and j∈[0,16) queries column u+8f at point v XOR j. In each case all seven bits of z survive once in the physical address; the three leading address bits are fixed and the low seven bits are XOR-translated. A row test may set f=0 since varying f simply permutes all 64 requested points at that seed. The row abscissas are permuted by adding a fixed field element for that single leaf, so RS membership is invariant under each such translation. A column test must include each fresh f when the whole family of candidate columns for a fixed seed is intended: f fixes the column and cannot be discarded as a reordering of the 16 points on a single column.

## Extending and recovering root polynomials

For an external unshifted polynomial P(X) in F[X] of degree <16, divide P(X+θ) by the monic q, writing R(X)+q(X)S(X). Both R and S have degree <8, and Q(X,Y)=R(X)+YS(X) supplies the bivariate proof. On shared parent cells x=βᵣ+u one has q(x)=yᵣ and Q(x,yᵣ)=P(x+θ). Extending by evaluation of Q on all 1,024 physical symbols therefore gives every row and column its required degree. The implementation performs this division, and computes leaf membership by interpolation in the extension field on the actual leaf points.

Conversely suppose *every* row and designated column is a codeword on the shared physical cells. Take two distinct ordinates y₀,y₁ and their row polynomials; affine interpolation of those two rows in Y produces Q(X,Y) of bidegree at most (7,1). At every common abscissa all rows agree with Q by the degree-one column condition. There are 32 common abscissas, so equality there determines each degree-<8 row everywhere. The root is consequently Q(X,q(X)) on θ+F, of degree ≤15. On substituting X+θ and projecting coefficients of E to their F-constant coordinate, evaluations at every point of F are preserved because root symbols lie in F. This reconstructs a degree-<16 external root polynomial.

The same argument gives a partial-agreement bound for this finite schedule. Write R for the number of failed rows and C for the number of failed columns on a fixed assignment. If there are at least two passing rows and eight passing common columns, interpolate any two of the passing rows in Y. Every passing row agrees with the resulting Q at all eight common abscissas and therefore on its entire domain. After coefficient projection, one external codeword differs from the root on at most 8R symbols (all eight parent sites in each failed row). Thus its relative root distance is ≤R/16. If fewer than two rows pass, a uniform root seed selects a failing row with probability ≥15/16. If fewer than eight columns pass, at least 25 columns fail. Those 25 failing columns occupy at least seven of the eight disjoint groups of four, so a seed checking its group rejects with probability at least 7/8. Any external F-word is within relative distance 7/8 of the degree-<16 code by interpolating through 16 coordinates. Consequently, in all cases the rejection probability of this RS schedule is at least the relative root distance, including the case of ≥15 failed rows. Equality at positive distance is possible: set all eight external symbols in row 0 to one, every other physical symbol to zero. Every nonzero degree-<16 polynomial agrees with at most 15 of the 120 zero entries and at most eight others. The zero polynomial thus attains minimum distance 8/128, exactly the rejection probability of the single failed row. Every noncode root rejects at least 8 of 128 seeds: some row or column must fail, and each such failure affects a full row or column group of seeds. A single changed parent cell also attains eight rejections. The outer ACSP predicate may reject additional seeds.

**Counterexample to sharing interpolation weights across all rows.** Take the honest external polynomial P(X)=X. The first eight symbols of row 1 are 8,…,15 at that row's eight parent abscissas. At w=32 the true symbol is 384 (the basis encoding of 3θ). Feeding these eight symbols into row 0's interpolation weights instead gives 392. In general both the interpolation nodes and targets must come from the requested row, or the decision circuit must explicitly justify an equivalent coordinate normalization for the whole leaf. The seed offset within a fixed leaf and the change of the affine domain between different row labels are distinct operations.
