# Exact soundness of the one-level NO instance

**Claim (written finite derivation).** The minimum number of rejected public seeds over all global field-proof assignments is 109 out of 128. In the Boolean compiler with an enforced true output and the two clamped public pins per bit, the minimum number of failed physical clause–seed pairs is also 109, counting template occurrences with multiplicity. The unrestricted field-proof claim permits arbitrary noncode root words and arbitrary auxiliary extension symbols.

## Which tests a seed sees

Use the parameter choice $k=7$, $a=3$ for the recursive B root, so $\ell=3$. Write a public seed as $(U,V)$ with three low bits and four high bits. The A base test reads all 128 of its symbols. For B, enumerating the fresh tapes at this seed tests **one** 64-symbol row indexed by $V$ and **four** 16-symbol columns indexed by $(U,A')$, one per two-bit auxiliary choice $A'$. Full leaf offsets reorder the inputs to those tests. They do not change the selected row or the four selected columns.

The row test demands a univariate extension-field polynomial of degree at most seven on the row abscissae. The column test demands an affine polynomial in the actual ordinate $Y=q(\beta)$, where $q(X)=\prod_{s\in L_0}(X+s)$, $|L_0|=8$. The 16 ordinates are distinct because $q$ is additive with kernel $L_0$. Every row contains all 32 common auxiliary abscissae and its own eight parent cells. The remaining row cells are also tested. The four columns in a group and the eight groups partition the 32 distinct common abscissae. These facts use the shared parent and sibling addresses in BV14 §6.1, equations (8)–(9), Proposition 6.1, and §6.2, with the field alignment of Expedition 038. [Ben-Sasson and Viola, *Short PCPs with projection queries*](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2014/017/download/)

## Why 20 accepted seeds are impossible

Fix **any** global root words and auxiliary assignment. A noncode A fails all seeds. Otherwise it agrees everywhere with one polynomial of degree at most one. If necessary translate the direct test from the shifted domain $\theta+F$ back to the external domain $F$. The characteristic-two extension does not affect the ensuing degree or root count.

Let $R$ be the number of rows passing their full test. Let $C$ be the number of low-coordinate groups for which all four columns pass. At most $RC$ seeds pass the B test, as the accepted coordinate pairs lie in that Cartesian product. If $R\le1$ or $C\le1$, at most 16 seeds can accept.

For $R\ge2$ and $C\ge2$, choose two passing rows. Write their degree-at-most-seven polynomials as $P_0(X),P_1(X)$ at their distinct ordinates $y_0,y_1$. Interpolate in $Y$ between these polynomials to form $Q(X,Y)$ with $\deg_X Q\le7$ and $\deg_Y Q\le1$. Each of the $4C\ge8$ columns in the passing groups is affine in $Y$ and agrees with $Q$ at $y_0$ and $y_1$, using physical equality of the row and column bits. It agrees at all 16 ordinates. For *each* passing row, its row polynomial and the corresponding $Q(X,y)$ therefore agree at at least eight distinct common $X$ values, giving equality on the entire row. This includes the eight physically shared root cells. We need no restriction on the parent values of failing rows.

On every potentially accepted seed $x$ the original $B(x)$ can now be replaced by

$$B_*(x)=Q(\theta+x,q(\theta+x)),$$

a single extension-field polynomial of degree at most $7+8=15$. This uses $q(\theta+x)=q(\beta)$ on the entire parent coset $\beta+L_0$. Apply the NO selector/quotient formula to the fixed degree-one A and $B_*$. The two singleton terms have degree at most six, the range term at most four, and the quotient term at most 19. Vanishing at zero would force $A(0)=1$ and vanishing at one would force $A(0)=0$, since the corresponding singleton selector is nonzero, the other selectors vanish there, and $Z_H$ vanishes on both. The residual is a nonzero polynomial over the extension field. Every accepting seed must be one of its at most 19 roots. The polynomial is allowed to disagree with the actual residual at rejected seeds.

For attainment take $A=0$ and the unique degree-at-most-15 polynomial $B$ with $B(x)=1/x$ at $x=4,\ldots,19$. A concrete formula uses $U(X)=\prod_{v=4}^{19}(X+v)$ and $B(X)=(U(X)+U(0))/(XU(0))$. Then the residual equals $Z_{\{1,2,3\}}(X)U(X)/U(0)$ and vanishes exactly at $1,\ldots,19$. Construct consistent auxiliary symbols from the bivariate representation of $B(X-\theta)$ in $X,q(X)$ by division with the monic degree-eight $q$. Both leaf codes pass at every seed. This supplies 109 rejections.

The Boolean gate-equivalence clauses force the verdict when the actual public bits and every ordinary clause are correct. A rejected field seed forces an ordinary failure at that seed or a wrong public cell at that seed. Each wrong public-bit cell causes two failed clamped pin pairs. Thus rejected seeds are at most ordinary failures plus half the pin failures. The extremal field proof with all public and gate cells set honestly fails exactly one true-output unit template at each rejection. Together these bounds give the physical minimum.

## Attacks exploiting the distinction between root membership and local tests

1. **A noncode can also attain the minimum.** Start from the extremizer above and toggle the low bit of the external B entry at $x=24$, retaining the original auxiliary symbols. This cannot be another degree-at-most-15 root polynomial: it agrees with the previous one at 127 points. Exactly the high row of 24 fails (one altered point in a 64-point row), and all columns still pass. All eight seeds in that row already lay outside the former acceptance set. All 19 previously accepted seeds continue to pass. For other choices of 16 interpolation points, choose a high row disjoint from the resulting acceptance set to obtain the same witness when such a row exists.
2. **Separate parent storage.** Use $A=0$ and the external $B(0)=0$, $B(x)=1/x$ for $x\ne0$. The ACSP accepts 127 seeds and fails only at zero. Keep the independent recursive root and all auxiliaries zero in the faulty layout; it accepts all 127 ACSP successes. With genuine redirects to the external B and the same zero auxiliary symbols, every row has at least one nonzero parent value and 56 zero other values. No row can be degree at most seven, so all 128 joined tests reject.
3. **Separate sibling storage.** Keep the inverse table at the genuinely shared parent. In each row interpolate its unique degree-at-most-seven polynomial through that row's eight parent points, and fill all of that row's extension cells from it. All row tests pass. Independent zero tables for the columns would then make 127 seeds accept. In the joined layout all 32 columns fail, so zero seeds accept.

For the last assertion, shift the row abscissa to $\xi=X-\theta$ and index parent rows by their cosets $v+L_0$ in the external space $F$. Put $y=q(v)$. On each of the 15 rows $y\ne0$, the interpolating polynomial at the shifted abscissa is

$$R_y(\xi)=\frac{q(\xi)}{y\,\xi}.$$
Here $q(0)=0$, the quotient has degree seven, and $q(\xi)=y$ on that parent row. At any common shifted abscissa $s$ outside $L_0$, the column values on these rows are $(q(s)/s)/y$, with $q(s)/s\ne0$. An affine column in the actual ordinate $q(\theta)+y$ would also be affine in $y$. Multiplying the claimed agreement by $y$ gives a nonzero polynomial of degree at most two vanishing at 15 distinct nonzero values, impossible. This argument does not depend on the assigned inverse value at zero or on the remaining row. A useful implementation invariant is that the 32 common abscissae are disjoint from every physical parent coset.

## Scope of the one-level argument

There is also a symbolic extension within this specially chosen family. Fix an integer $a\ge3$, take $k=2a+1$, $s=2^a$, a field $F$ of size $2^k=2s^2$, and the same NO singleton/range/quotient gadgets on $H=\{0,1,2,3\}$ in binary coordinates. Keep A's cutoff two and set B's cutoff to $2^{k-a}=2s$. Use the shifted root and shared cells of Expedition 038 and enumerate all fresh tapes at each seed. The row-child dimension is $2a$ and the column-child dimension is $a+1$, so both stop at leaves. Conversely, for fixed recursion rate $a$, a non-leaf with both children leaves must have $k>2a$ and $\lfloor k/2\rfloor+a\le2a$, forcing $k=2a+1$.

There are $2s$ rows, with row degree less than $s$, and $s$ low-coordinate groups of $s/2$ columns each, with column degree less than two. Each seed tests one row and one entire group. If fewer than two rows or two groups pass, at most $2s$ seeds can accept. Otherwise interpolate a degree-one function of $Y$ between two passing rows as above. Two passing groups give at least $s$ distinct common abscissae, determining every passing row polynomial. Since the parent coset vanisher now has degree $s$, the common lift has degree at most $(s-1)+s=2s-1$. The selector/quotient residual has degree at most $2s+3$, and still cannot vanish at both zero and one. Thus at most $2s+3$ seeds accept in either case. There are enough nonzero addresses outside $H$ to choose $2s$ distinct points. Take the quotient interpolating $1/x$ there, with $A=0$ and consistent auxiliary cells. Its residual roots are exactly those $2s$ points together with $1,2,3$, attaining the bound. The minimum is consequently $2s^2-2s-3$ rejected seeds, also the failed-pair minimum with the stated gate compiler. This last formula is a written derivation for this designed one-level family. The executable combined fixture here takes $a=3$. It supplies no estimate for two or more recursive levels or for arbitrary source instances.
