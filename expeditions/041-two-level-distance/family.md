# The two-level family and the uniform constant in the original schedule

This is a symbolic extension of the shared-cell RS argument. Its only numerical full-oracle instance in this expedition is $(k,a)=(8,3)$. Fix any integer $a\ge3$, let $s=2^a$, and use the recursion with root dimension $k>2a$, child dimensions $\lfloor k/2\rfloor+a$ (row) and $\lceil k/2\rceil$ (column), and stopping rule that a dimension at most $2a$ is a leaf. The maximum child dimension is the row dimension. The root has depth exactly two precisely when its largest child dimension is $2a+1$: the only internal dimension whose two children are leaves is $2a+1$, and any dimension larger than $2a+1$ still has a non-leaf child. This gives the two values of $k$ below. [BV14 §6.1–6.2](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2014/017/download/)

| Root dimension | Number of outer rows $m$ | Outer-column degree cutoff $t=m/s$ | Root code dimension $D=2m$ | Number of seeds $N$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $2a+2$ | $2s$ | 2 | $4s$ | $4s^2$ |
| $2a+3$ | $4s$ | 4 | $8s$ | $8s^2$ |

A degree cutoff is strict. The external field is $F=\mathbb F_{2^k}$, with $N=2sm$ field points and root code $\deg<D=N/s$. Write $z=u+2sv$ for $0\le u<2s$, $0\le v<m$, in binary coordinates, and put $u=sp+b$ where $p\in\{0,1\}$ and $0\le b<s$. The outer column child has dimension $\log_2 m\le a+2\le2a$ even for the odd value of $k$. The outer row child has dimension $2a+1$ in both cases, with inner leaf dimensions $2a$ and $a+1$. Use the correctly shared field labels and sufficient extension-field dimension to select the prescribed independent basis at each node. Coordinates of the field used at an inner node can be those of the common ambient extension. An arbitrary extension proof is allowed.

## Pairing and agreement

At $(v,u)$ the original schedule tests a group of $s/2$ outer common columns, indexed by the full outer low part $u$ and fresh bits. In the selected outer row the inherited words have index $u+2sf$ for $0\le f<s$; the inner row labels they select are $j=p+2f$. There are $2s$ possible inner rows. The inner column group indexed by $b$ has $s/2$ columns. Requiring the original tests at $u$ **and** $u\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}(s+1)$ fixes $v$, reverses $p$ and changes $b$. A paired success therefore checks all $2s$ inner rows and $s$ distinct common inner columns. Any two inner rows determine a bivariate polynomial of bidegrees $(<s,<2)$ by interpolating in the ordinate. Its column agrees with each passing common column at those two ordinates, hence at every ordinate. The $s$ common abscissae now fix the entire polynomial on each inner row. Composing with the inner subspace vanisher of degree $s$ gives a degree-$<2s$ polynomial on the **whole** selected outer row. The points use the actual node basis; this reasoning never identifies abscissae from two different inner geometries.

Let $S$ be the paired success set for a fixed global assignment. Write $R$ and $U$ for its projection to outer-row and low-coordinate labels. The involution on $u$ preserves $S$ row by row, so $|U|$ is even. If $|R|<t$, then $|S|\le(t-1)2s<D$. If $|U|\le2$, then $|S|\le2m=D$. In either case interpolate a degree-$<D$ external polynomial through all sites in $S$. These sparse cases also cover empty $S$.

Otherwise recover the entire low-degree outer row at every $v\in R$. Interpolate $t$ of those row polynomials in their distinct outer ordinates to obtain $Q$ of bidegrees $(<2s,<t)$. There are $|U|\ge4$ passing groups of outer columns and thus at least $4(s/2)=2s$ passing common abscissae. Each such column is of degree $<t$; its agreement with $Q$ at the $t$ chosen row ordinates forces equality on all outer row ordinates. At each recovered row the $2s$ common abscissae force equality on the entire row, including its $2s$ properly aliased parent symbols. For outer subspace vanisher $q_0$ of degree $2s$, the degree of $Q(X,q_0(X))$ is at most $(2s-1)+2s(t-1)=D-1$. Translate by the root shift, then use an $F$-linear coefficient projection from the ambient field onto $F$ fixing $F$. This produces one genuine external codeword agreeing on all of $S$. In both cases the paired rejection probability is at least the relative distance $\delta$ of the root to its RS code.

For sharpness, set the $2s$ external symbols of one entire outer coset to one and all other physical symbols to zero. Only the inner rows $j=0,1$ of that outer row contain external changes. Each contains $s$ ones at its parent sites and $s^2-s$ zeros on the rest of its domain, violating the strict degree-$s$ test. The outer common columns have no changed cells. Every seed in the affected outer coset selects one of these two inner rows; no other seed rejects in either schedule. A nonzero external polynomial of degree $<D$ can agree on at most $D-1$ zero entries and at most $2s$ one entries, fewer than the $N-2s$ agreements of the zero polynomial. Hence both paired rejection and distance equal $2s/N=1/m>0$.

## A uniform upper bound on possible unpaired constants

For every outer row $v$, set $d_v=v\bmod 2$. At an external root point $x$ labeled by $(v,u)$ store $d_v$ if $p=d_v$, and store $x^D$ if $p\ne d_v$. At every non-root site of the outer row let the value be its own low-part parity $p$ (bit $a$ of its coordinate). In each inner row give every free extension site the value $d_v$. All duplicate names are routed to their actual parent or row sibling. This is a legal assignment.

Every designated outer common column is constant in $v$: its fixed low-coordinate bit $p$ provides its value even as its row label varies. Every inner common column is constant $d_v$. A selected inner row with $p=d_v$ has all its parent and extension values equal to $d_v$. Thus all seeds with $p=d_v$ pass. For $p\ne d_v$, enumerating the fresh tapes includes a $j=p+2f$ with $f\ne0$. Its $s$ parent positions are outer extension sites, all equal to $p$, and its $s^2-s$ remaining entries equal $d_v$. A polynomial of degree $<s$ agreeing with the latter entries must be constant $d_v$, so this inner row fails. The original rejection probability is exactly $1/2$ for both values of $k$.

Half of the root table consists of Frobenius values, and the two forced constant regions have $N/4$ sites apiece because $m$ is even. For a nonconstant candidate $P$ of degree $<D$, the nonzero polynomials $P$, $P+1$ and $P+X^D$ have degrees at most $D-1,D-1,D$. Counting their roots separately on the three disjoint regions gives at most $3D-2$ agreements. The map $x\mapsto x^D$ is bijective on $F$ since $D$ is a power of two. A constant therefore has at most $N/4+1$ agreements for arbitrary external labeling. In the polynomial-basis labeling with its first direction equal to one, the sites $x=0,1$ are both in the forced-zero region. Their Frobenius values 0 and 1 occur nowhere in the Frobenius region. Consequently the largest agreement for constants is **exactly $N/4$**, attained by 0 and 1. This labeling is sufficient for a counterexample to a uniform guarantee.

Thus $\delta\ge 1-\max\{N/4,3D-2\}/N$ and the constructed root has $\delta\le3/4$. For $a\ge4$, $N/4=sD/4\ge4D>3D-2$, so $\delta=3/4$ exactly and the original ratio $\Pr[\mathrm{reject}]/\delta$ equals **$2/3$**, not merely a limit. Any universal constant in an original-schedule lower bound over this family is therefore at most $2/3$. For $a=3$, the root-count cap is 94 of 256 in the even case and 190 of 512 in the odd case. Both still yield strict violations of unit distance soundness. The paired inequality and the union bound give the universal lower constant $1/2$ for the original schedule; the gap between $1/2$ and $2/3$ remains.

## Costs and boundary

The paired schedule uses the same $k$ public seed bits, one fixed XOR mask and at most twice the original leaf-word reads and predicate work. Translation preserves every fixed-slot fiber size. These statements compare the two tests at the **same** $a,k$. As $a$ grows, each seed in the unpaired schedule selects $s$ inner rows of $s^2$ symbols, $s/2$ inner columns of $2s$ symbols and $s/2$ outer columns of $m$ symbols, even after pooling repeated full leaves. In particular the $s^3$ inner-row symbol entries alone grow exponentially in $k$ along this exactly-two-level family. The calculation supplies neither a uniform poly$(k)$-query bound for growing $a$ nor a recursion argument for trees with three or more levels. It establishes a property of the specified RS test and its root distance, rather than single-clause rejection or a general complexity lower bound.
