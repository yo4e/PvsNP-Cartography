# A four-translation repair at root dimension ten or eleven

**Status.** Written extension of the paired agreement argument at exponent $a=3$ for the specified shared-cell schedule. The combined field verifier and sharp NO count executed elsewhere in this expedition are at $k=8$. This section does not report an executed $k=10$ or $k=11$ field verifier.

Use the same BV14 seed partition, fresh-tape enumeration, finite-field row/column domains and canonical physical redirects as in the [two-level analysis](paired-soundness.md). Choose $F=\mathbb F_{2^k}$, $k=10$ or $11$, and an ambient extension with enough independent auxiliary basis directions at each internal node. At root coordinate $z=u+32v$, with five-bit low part $u$, require all four RS predicate values on the **same** proof at
$$
(v,u\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}h),\qquad h\in H=\{0,9,18,27\}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb F_2}\{9,18\}.
$$
These four seeds differ only in their low part. At both root dimensions the selected row child has dimension $5+3=8$, and its dimension-seven row grandchildren recurse once more. The root column child has dimension $k-5$, at most the leaf threshold six. [Ben-Sasson and Viola, §6.1–6.2, equations (8)–(9), Proposition 6.1](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2014/017/download/)

| Root dimension | Outer row/group labels | Outer-column degree cutoff | Root code dimension $D$ | Number of root coordinates |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10 | 32 rows, 32 low labels | 4 | 128 | 1,024 |
| 11 | 64 rows, 32 low labels | 8 | 256 | 2,048 |

Each low label selects four distinct outer common columns. Row polynomial degree cutoff is 32 in both cases.

## What one four-way success certifies inside a selected row

1. For each shift $h$ and each outer fresh word $f=0,\ldots,7$, the dimension-eight child receives the inherited word $s_1=(u\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}h)+32f$. Its selected dimension-seven row label is $j=\lfloor s_1/16\rfloor=\mathrm{bit}_4(u\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}h)+2f$. For any **one fixed shift**, this supplies eight labels of one parity. Over all four shifts both parities appear, giving all 16 labels.
2. Fix such a $j$. The two shifts yielding its parity differ by XOR 9 (the two pairs are $\{0,9\}$ and $\{18,27\}$). They use the same $f=\lfloor j/2\rfloor$. The low four bits entering this branch differ by XOR 9, toggling both the parity bit and one low residue bit for the next split. For each of the two shifts, all eight fresh words $g$ at the dimension-eight row edge are available, so the dimension-seven row receives the complete lists of seeds $((u\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}h)\bmod16)+16g$. The resulting selected leaf tests cover all 16 inner rows (strict degree eight) and eight distinct common inner columns (strict degree two). Interpolating two rows in the ordinate and then all the rows on these eight common abscissae recovers an actual degree-$<16$ polynomial on the **whole** dimension-seven word, including its shared parent sites. This works for every $j$.
3. Return to the dimension-eight child. Its selected common column groups have labels $(u\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}h)\bmod16$ for $h\in H$. These four low nibbles are distinct, because $H$ restricts modulo 16 to $\{0,9,2,11\}$. Consequently 16 distinct common columns, each of degree $<2$ on its true sixteen ordinates, passed all their leaf tests. Interpolating any two of the 16 recovered rows in the ordinate and applying the root bound on the 16 common abscissae gives a single bivariate polynomial of bidegrees $(<16,<2)$ on all parent cells. Substitution of the local degree-16 subspace vanisher yields a univariate polynomial of degree at most $15+16=31$ on the entire dimension-eight word. This gives the required outer-row polynomial from just one four-way success in its row $v$.

The two round-trip interpolations use each node's own abscissae and ordinate function. They use actual physical equality at every row/sibling intersection and shared parent site, including nested returns. They do not assume that independent low-degree polynomials on unrelated row bases use the same coefficients.

## Agreement across outer rows

For one global field-proof assignment let $S$ be its four-way success set, $R$ its set of active row labels, and $U$ its set of active low labels. Let $m=2^{k-5}$ and $t=m/8$, so $D=4m=32t$. Each accepted row is completely recovered as above. The four-way condition is invariant under translating $u$ by any $h\in H$, at each fixed $v$. Hence $U$ is a union of complete size-four cosets.

If $|R|<t$, at most $32(t-1)<D$ coordinates can accept. If $|U|\le4$, at most $4m=D$ can accept. Choose $D$ interpolation sites containing $S$. The resulting external $F$-polynomial of degree $<D$ matches the actual symbols at every accepting seed. Otherwise recover at least $t$ full outer row polynomials and at least eight distinct passing groups of columns, because the size of $U$ is a multiple of four. Their 32 or more distinct common abscissae suffice to determine a degree-$<32$ outer row. Interpolate $t$ recovered rows in their distinct ordinates. Every good common column agrees with the resulting bivariate polynomial at all ordinates by its degree-$<t$ test; consequently every recovered row agrees everywhere on its own domain. Composition with the root degree-32 subspace vanisher has degree at most $31+32(t-1)=D-1$. Translate back by the root shift, then project coefficients $F$-linearly to $F$ fixing $F$. Every root agreement on $S$ survives. Therefore the four-way rejection probability is at least the root's relative distance $\delta$ from the external degree-$<D$ RS code.

To attain equality at positive distance, set the 32 parent symbols in one entire outer coset to one, and every other physical symbol to zero. The zero polynomial has exactly 32 errors. A nonzero degree-$<D$ polynomial can agree on at most $D-1$ zero sites and 32 one sites, fewer than $2^k-32$. Thus $\delta=32/2^k$. Every original seed in the changed outer coset checks a failing innermost row: take fresh $f=0$ at the root row step and $g=0$ at the next row step. The outer child's row label $j=\mathrm{bit}_4(u)$ selects one of the two 16-site blocks containing parent ones, and the innermost row label $\mathrm{bit}_3(u)$ selects one of its two blocks of eight ones. That full length-64 leaf has eight actual parent ones and 56 extension zeros, so it fails the degree-$<8$ test. All outer common columns still contain zero everywhere, and all subtrees of other outer rows are zero. Thus the original and four-way tests each reject exactly those 32 seeds. The four-way upper estimate by a union bound in turn gives $p_{\mathrm{original}}\ge\delta/4$ for these two topologies; no independence is used and this need not be the best lower constant.

The modification needs no extra independent public coins and at most four times the original leaf reads and predicate evaluations, with the same choice of field, $k$, and $a$. A fixed shift preserves the projection locality of each raw oracle query. The argument concerns the enumerated-tape RS predicate in these two layouts. In particular it does not say that four translations are necessary, establish any two-translation impossibility, or yield a multi-depth formula by induction.
