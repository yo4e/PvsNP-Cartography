# When two recursive levels lose distance, and how to recover it

The original two-level RS verifier can reject only half of all public seeds on a root at distance at least 162/256 from the nearest codeword. Each outer row can pass its own half of the inner tests, while sharing all its physical cells. The previous night's example had distance zero. This one refutes the inequality itself. [Construction and distance certificate](counterexample.md)

For the repaired verifier, test the same proof at the original seed and at its fixed XOR translate. Choose a mask that changes both the parity and the column residue in the child. A paired success reconstructs an entire outer row, and the outer columns force compatible rows to come from a single root polynomial. In the sparse case a polynomial can interpolate every accepting site directly. Paired rejection dominates root distance with sharp coefficient one. Taking a union bound gives coefficient one half for the original predicate. The written argument covers precisely the two-level families. An unmodified family member at every rate exponent at least four can reject exactly one half at distance exactly three quarters. The best common coefficient for the original test lies between one half and two thirds. [Finite paired argument](paired-soundness.md) · [Both parameter families and costs](family.md)
