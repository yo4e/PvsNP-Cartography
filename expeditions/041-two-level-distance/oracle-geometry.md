# Physical table and execution of the two-level verifier

The external alphabet F is GF(256) in the polynomial basis modulo t⁸+t⁴+t³+t+1 (0x11b). The internal alphabet is E=F[θ]/(θ²+θ+0x20), represented by a+256b for a+bθ. The absolute trace of 0x20 is one, as checked by summing its eight Frobenius conjugates in F. This gives a quadratic field extension. Embed the root on θ+F, so the external cell at x always contains an F symbol even though its domain abscissa is θ+x.

At a node with shift s and ordered binary basis D of dimension d, let l=floor(d/2), U=span(D[:l]), and q(X)=∏(X+u) over u∈U. The high-coordinate v chooses β=s+high(v). The row has ordinate q(β), shift β and ordered basis consisting of the l low directions followed by c₁,c₂,c₀+β. Its common-rectangle points have last bit one. The root uses (c₀,c₁,c₂)=(2θ,4θ,8θ). Each outer row uses (16θ,32θ,64θ) as its own three auxiliary vectors. These choices are independent of that node's root/row shift and directions: the first-level high halves are supported on bit positions 0 through 3 and the second-level auxiliary vectors use positions 4 through 6. Both U's lie in the external F basis. This instantiates the affine-domain split, bivariate grid and leaf test of Ben-Sasson and Viola, §6.1, equations (6)–(9), Proposition 6.1 and Definition 6.2. [Primary paper](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2014/017/download/)

**Physical addresses.** A word at outer row v and coordinate w has ID 256⌊w/16⌋+16v+(w mod 16). The first 256 addresses are the actual root cells. Outer column c reads the shared row slot 64+c. At the second level, inner row j in outer row v has leaf slot i. Slots 0≤i<8 redirect to outer(v,8j+i), which can itself redirect to the root. Other slots have ID 2048+896v+128(⌊i/8⌋−1)+8j+(i mod 8). Inner column c reads its row sibling at i=32+c. Overall there are 16,384 physical words: 256 external and 16,128 extension. The checker compares the field abscissas at each geometric overlap and only allocates one cell for each shared symbol.

**Seed schedule.** The outer seed is z=u+16v. The four outer column branches f query column u+16f, with its 16 points enumerated in offset order from v. The eight outer row fresh words f supply child seed t=u+16f. Inside this row the inherited low index b=t mod 8 selects columns b+8h (h=0,…,3). The inherited high index j=⌊t/8⌋ selects an inner row; its leaf coordinate is b+8h (h=0,…,7) before XORing each of the 64 offsets. Inner column leaves enumerate all 16 points starting at inherited j. The code enumerates all fresh control settings. Each distinct fixed leaf and incoming leaf coordinate yields a permutation of the whole leaf, so a full test can be evaluated once in absolute order. No whole-outer-row acceptance flag is cached. The actual abscissas for each inner row vary with its geometry.

**Fixtures.** In addition to the zero proof, the positive control fills the roots with P(x)=x³¹, then interpolates each outer row from its 16 overlapping root values, and each inner row from its eight overlapping outer values. This correctly reconstructs a legal proof: for the root P(X+θ), divide by the monic q of degree 16, obtaining a bivariate representation of separate degrees <16 and <2. Its restrictions to the rows interpolate the specified 16 values; their common columns are affine in q(β). The same reasoning inside the rows uses a monic q of degree eight and separate degree bounds <8 and <2. The executable checks both stages against actual row and column memberships. A sharp small-distance control sets one full outer parent coset to one, every other word to zero. Exactly 16 seeds reject, and its root distance is 16 because a nonzero degree-<32 polynomial cannot agree with more than 31 of the 240 zero entries and 16 others.

For the counterexample, write dᵥ=v mod 2. On root z=16v+u put dᵥ if bit 3 of u equals dᵥ; use z³² in F otherwise. This latter value is (X+θ)³² at the physical abscissa X=θ+z. Every other outer symbol in row v at slot w is bit 3 of w. Every independent inner auxiliary symbol in the same subtree is dᵥ. Both sets of columns are constant along each column. The checker obtains all pass/fail decisions by field interpolation through the first two (column) or eight (inner-row) points and comparison at all remaining points. Four fixtures check all 832 leaves and every seed; the paired schedule tests the complete predicates at z and z XOR 9. The saved XOR-mask sweep additionally covers every shift of the low four seed bits. These are finite checks of the specified oracle schedule. They do not count clauses of an outer Boolean compilation.
