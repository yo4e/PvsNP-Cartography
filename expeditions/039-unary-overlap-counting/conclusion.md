# Conclusion • a sharp finite verifier and a costed unary route

**Finite verifier.** A YES and a NO translated-clause instance over GF(32) now have executable bit-level verifiers. Each produces 469,056 clause–seed pairs. The YES proof passes all of them. On NO, every global proof fails at least 25 pairs, and a particular cubic quotient attains exactly 25. The lower bound uses a nonzero residual of degree at most seven and Boolean gate/pin semantics. The upper bound was checked on all the generated physical clauses. This base case terminates at both Reed–Solomon roots. [Construction and witnesses](base-case.md) · [Generated results](base-acsp-checks.json)

**Unary budget.** Replicating the canonical proof across external padding preserves the optimum degree-d error exactly. For the published projection-clause interface, encoding answers by occurrence and checking their common physical addresses avoids a maximum query-density assumption. Guess one low-degree local table per occurrence, sum the failed clauses, and charge discordant pairs by the smaller endpoint mass. Every local guess on NO then costs at least the global failure threshold. On YES an approximation of uniform error at most 1/(4J·2^{ceil(log₂ J)}) meets the split-CNF threshold, with J literal occurrences. Polynomially many low-degree counts still fit the unary hierarchy clock at sufficiently small degree coefficient. [Weighted overlap argument](weighted-overlap.md) · [Resource and quantifier ledger](budget.md)

A second construction applies line interpolation to a low-degree extension of the same canonical proof. It produces a different encoded Boolean function and needs no occurrence gluing. Uniformly low bit error on the extended cube yields a distribution of decoded polynomials with small error at each physical address, using one shared line direction. Both derivations concern infinitely many occupied YES lengths, degree on the order of input length divided by its logarithm and an inverse-polynomial uniform error threshold. Their mathematical status here is a written candidate argument using external theorems. The finite enumeration is separate evidence. [Derivation and primary-source comparison](line-interpolation.md)

**Decision.** Issue 29's requested budget and conditional implication are worked out. Park the inverse-polynomial existence question as a stand-alone novelty target. The published rigidity results already give constant uniform error in a different degree range, and the interpolation route uses standard local reductions. Constant error at the degree of this ledger remains outside these estimates. Issue 28's next conformance target is a generated verifier with at least one recursive child, physical parent/sibling aliases and both honest and adversarial global assignments.
