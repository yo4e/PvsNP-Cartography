# A second route using line interpolation

**Scope.** This written comparison obtains an infinitely-often inverse-polynomial uniform-error obstruction for an **encoded** proof function from the published verifier and counting lemmas. It uses a different function from the raw-address and slot-table functions. It gives no uniform constant-error bound at the same degree, assertion of priority, or Lean verification.

## Start with the published verifier

Use the same unary hierarchy language and clocks as in [the budget](budget.md). The latter bound at $n+1$ is $o(2^n)$. Any fixed polynomial-in-$n$ simulation overhead can be paid from the strengthened time saving below. Apply Viola TR20-015 rev. 5, Lemma 11, on input $1^n$. Let $r\le n+O(\log n)$ be the common seed/address width, $J=\operatorname{poly}(n)$ the number of three-literal clause types, and $n^{-b}$ an eventual lower bound on the NO rejection gap. Fix an integer $b\ge1$ large enough for this bound. The published query maps are projections. No rank or smoothness of their marginal distributions is required.

On YES let $\pi$ be the lexicographically first global proof satisfying all seed-clause tests. On NO use the zero table. One can explicitly produce the conjunction of all tests in exponential time, then recover each desired proof bit by lexicographic prefix search with a SAT oracle. Both the number of queries and the length of each are at most $2^{O(n)}$, so this takes $2^{O(n)}$ time with an NP oracle. The same proof is used for every entry of the encoding.

For clarity, the intermediate inference from Viola §2.3 is a *pointwise*, high-precision one. If a single input-independent distribution on degree-$d$ polynomials over the proof addresses is wrong with probability at most $n^{-(b+1)}$ at each address, then each honest clause fails with probability at most $3 n^{-(b+1)}$. Averaging over one shared polynomial gives a fixed proof polynomial with this aggregate failure. Consequently a degree cap $d=\lfloor c n/\log_2 n\rfloor$, with sufficiently small $c>0$, cannot provide such a pointwise approximation at all large YES lengths. In the paper's binary-input notation the canonical function has separate $x,y$ inputs of combined length $2n+O(\log n)$; hardwiring $x$ preserves its **pointwise** guarantee. Our unary convention makes the instance implicit in an injective length. One can pad the raw table to $2n+C$ ignoring redundant bits, with small exceptions tabulated, to get a single E^NP family expressing this intermediate inference. Theorem 3 as printed is the different, constant-pointwise-error $\Omega(n/\log^2 n)$ result. The high-precision inference uses the unamplified counting argument, not a strengthening deduced just by changing the printed theorem's error.

## Encode and decode

For all large $n$, put $h=\lceil\log_2(n+2)\rceil$, $u=\max(1,\lceil r/h\rceil)$, $D=u(2^h-1)$, and $w=\lceil\log_2(D+2)\rceil$. Over the field $F=\mathbb F_{2^w}$ set $q=2^w$, so $q-1>D$ and $q=O(n^2)$. Find a monic irreducible binary degree-$w$ polynomial deterministically by enumeration and trial division through degree $\lfloor w/2\rfloor$. This is polynomial in $n$ since $w=O(\log n)$. In the resulting polynomial basis use the span $H$ of its first $h$ basis elements, including 1. It is a set of size $2^h$; a subfield is unnecessary.

Break an $r$-bit address into $u$ blocks of $h$ bits, appending zero bits in its last block. View the blocks as elements of $H$. On $H^u$ define the table by ignoring those appended coordinates and looking up the original proof. Tensor-product Lagrange interpolation gives a field polynomial $L:F^u\to F$ agreeing with this Boolean table, of individual degree at most $2^h-1$ and total degree at most $D$. Evaluation by summing all grid Lagrange terms costs $2^{hu}\operatorname{poly}(n)=2^{O(n)}$ elementary operations and at most exponentially many calls to the E^NP evaluator above, still in E^NP.

Let $\lambda:F\to\mathbb F_2$ extract the constant basis coefficient, so $\lambda(1)=1$. Encode the single bit $g(v)=\lambda(L(v))$. Use injective length $m=8n+C$ with a fixed constant $C$ large enough: $uw\le(2+o(1))n$ and $w=O(\log n)$; unused input bits are ignored. Read zero at other lengths. An approximating $m$-bit polynomial of uniform error $\eta$ has some fixed suffix restriction of error at most $\eta$ on the actual $uw$ uniform coordinates, by averaging. Fix that restriction $P$. Requiring the zero suffix to be good would be an error.

For every $x\in H^u$ representing a proof address and direction $a\in F^u$,
```
              π(x) = XOR_{t ∈ F*} g(x + t a).
```
Here $F^*=F\setminus\{0\}$. Indeed $S(t)=L(x+t a)$ has degree at most $D<q-1$. Its constant term is the sum of its $q-1$ nonzero evaluations: $q-1=1$ in characteristic two, and $\sum_{t\ne0}t^j=0$ for $1\le j<q-1$, by cyclicity of $F^*$. Apply the linear functional $\lambda$. This simplification removes symbol-bit tags. If instead one chooses $D+1$ arbitrary nonzero interpolation points and general field weights, one can store all $w$ symbol bits with $a_0=\lceil\log_2 w\rceil$ tag bits. The sum of the uniform symbol-bit error rates over the valid tags is at most $2^{a_0}\eta$, hence the upper bound would be $(D+1)2^{a_0}\eta$.

Replace $g$ by $P$ and choose **one** direction $a$ uniformly from all $F^u$, including zero. For each fixed $x$ and $t\ne0$, the point $x+t a$ is uniform on $F^u$. A union bound gives error at most $(q-1)\eta$ on that fixed $x$, without independence among the queries. For a fixed $a$, their XOR is a single Boolean polynomial in the original address of degree at most $d=\deg P$: each argument is a fixed translation of an affine bit embedding and XOR preserves degree. Average the clause failures over that shared direction and all verifier tests; a single fixed decoded polynomial has failure at most $3(q-1)\eta$ on YES. There is no need for one direction to decode *all* addresses correctly, and no decoding on NO is used.

The strict cutoff $D<q-1$ is necessary for this summation identity. Already in GF(4), $S(t)=t^3$ has value zero at zero but sum one on all nonzero points. In contrast, for $S(t)=a+bt+ct^2$ the same sum is exactly $a$ for every choice of coefficients.

## Price the simulation

If $\eta\le n^{-(b+4)}$, then $3(q-1)\eta< n^{-b}/3$ for large $n$. The simulator guesses an arbitrary degree-$d$ proof polynomial on **$r$ original address bits**. It does not enumerate the directions, encoded addresses, approximants, or NP queries. For each clause type it constructs the signed failure polynomial of degree at most $3d$ in $r$ seed variables and counts its ones using Viola Theorem 12, handling a constant polynomial directly. Accept if fewer than $J2^r/(2n^b)$ tests fail, with exact integer cross-multiplication. All guessed proofs on NO fail at least $J2^r n^{-b}$ tests.

Choose $d=\lfloor c n/\log_2 n\rfloor$. The dense guess has at most $B=\sum_{i\le d}\binom{r}{i}=2^{o(n)}$ bits. Expanding substitutions of complemented coordinates and multiplying three expressions takes at most $(2^d B+1)^3\operatorname{poly}(n)=2^{o(n)}$ time. The counting calls together cost $J2^{r-\Omega(r/(3d))+O(\log r)}$. For $r<n/2$, brute force after construction takes $2^{n/2+o(n)}$; otherwise $r/d\ge (\log_2 n)/(2c)$. Taking $c$ sufficiently small beats the $O(\log n)$ address overhead, all polynomial factors, and an arbitrary fixed extra $K\log_2 n$, giving time $O(2^n/n^K)$. The sum uses $O(r+\log J)$-bit counters. Thus valid uniform approximants at all sufficiently large YES lengths would contradict the fixed unary hierarchy. Finitely many exceptional small instances can be recognized by a finite table.

It follows from this written composition that the encoded E^NP family has infinitely many occupied YES lengths $m=8n+C$ on which every degree-$\lfloor\alpha m/\log_2 m\rfloor$ Boolean polynomial has uniform error greater than $m^{-(b+4)}$, for sufficiently small fixed $\alpha>0$. NO and unused lengths have the zero table. This derivation establishes neither the same precision on the original proof cube nor a constant-error result. It demonstrates why a numerical existence tradeoff alone is insufficient to attribute novelty to an address-specific construction.

## Primary-source comparison

- [Viola, TR20-015 revision 5](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2020/015/revision/5/download/), Lemmas 10–11, Theorem 12, §2.3 pp. 13–15. Sections 4–5 address a different uniform-error target. Lemma 23 obtains a constant-error polynomial lower bound conditional on a circuit-size upper bound for E^NP. Section 1.4 compares the constant-error uniform rigidity tradeoff of Bhangale–Harsha–Paradise–Tal, which gives degree on the order of $n/(\log n\log\log n)$ by counting monomials. Neither is an identification of the canonical function here.
- [Beaver, Feigenbaum, Kilian & Rogaway, *Locally Random Reductions: Improvements and Applications*](https://www.cs.ucdavis.edu/~rogaway/papers/lrr.pdf), printed pp. 5–6, Theorem 3, particularly privacy, reconstruction by Lagrange interpolation at zero, and linearity. Taking their privacy parameter one gives random lines. The use of all nonzero points and the displayed characteristic-two circuit-degree accounting are spelled out above.
- [Williams, *Improving Exhaustive Search Implies Superpolynomial Lower Bounds*](https://people.csail.mit.edu/rrw/improved-algs-lbs2.pdf), Theorems 2.1–2.2, gives the fixed unary time-hierarchy formulation and discusses machine-model simulation overhead.
- Searches of the primary papers above, [Huang–Viola](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00224-026-10268-9) and [Chen–Lyu](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2021/003/) did not identify this exact encoded-projection-PCP statement as an explicitly labeled published theorem. The low-degree-extension mechanism and the counting route each have prior art. This limited comparison does not support a novelty claim.

For the one-bit full-line decoder the union bound is sharp as a statement about unrestricted errors: flip one point v distinct from the anchor x in an all-zero extension. Exactly q−1 directions (v−x)/t encounter it, once each. In GF(8)² the uniform error 1/64 gives pointwise error 7/64. The finite program checks this, the degree cutoff, and all 4,096 honest target/point/direction triples for this decoder in addition to the tagged tests.
