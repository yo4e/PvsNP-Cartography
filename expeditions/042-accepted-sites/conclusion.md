# Exact accepted-site agreement fails

Two honest recursive Reed–Solomon proofs can be spliced on the physical cells read at chosen seeds. With fixed rate and polynomially many fixed XOR translates, every sufficiently large instance admits a shared proof for which each low-degree polynomial misses some accepting external site. In a designed unsatisfiable field constraint, a splice can also exceed the maximum number of residual roots available to an honest polynomial. The [address-overlap argument](planting.md) and [outer construction](outer-planting.md) are written derivations.

At 256 seeds the [physical replay](physical_patch.py) first accepts one planted one and 120 zeros. The nearest root polynomial has eight differences on the full word and just one on the accepting sites. A second proof splices two degree-31 reciprocal interpolants. Exactly 67 seeds satisfy both the unpaired proximity check and the designed NO constraint, against a maximum of 35 for genuine polynomials. Mask pair 0,1 still accepts 67, while the previously repaired pair 0,9 accepts zero on this particular proof. These are actual counts for a specific global assignment. [Exact output](physical-patch.json)

The reach of the obstruction depends on how many distinct seeds can read one canonical address. The root-count discrepancy merits quantifying the best achievable *approximate* agreement and its predicate cost. The finite example alone cannot decide the arbitrary-depth question.

## What survives

- At any depth and fixed rate, a proof-independent polynomial-sized set of fixed XOR masks gives one polynomial agreeing on at least a 1−ε fraction of accepting external sites for any inverse-polynomial ε. The proof treats sparse sets by interpolation and dense sets by centered mixing from the existing distance estimate. [Agreement and raw-read budget](accepted-fraction.md)
- Evaluating a NO residual once and amplifying only the two proximity predicates permits joint acceptance at most b+η when legal polynomial tuples give at most bN roots. The explicit parameter choice at b=1/4 and η=1/8 gives at least 5/8 rejection on field seeds. In the single-output Boolean encoding the clause denominator is real: minimizing over gate and pin tables at fixed field proof yields exactly the field rejection divided by the new clause count. [Derivation and prior art](outer-and-sampler.md)
- In the unchanged two-level schedule the sharp uniform rejection-to-distance coefficient is 2/3. Three codewords suffice to cover its accepting sites. For the designed unpaired GF(256) NO fixture the number of joint rejections lies between **154 and 189 inclusive** at optimum: 154 follows from the cover bound, and 189 is attained by the 67-acceptance proof above. The optimum in that interval is open. The paired optimum was 221. [Parity reconstruction, proof and scope](two-level.md) · [Nonconstant physical reconstruction](parity-recovery-checks.json)

The generic derivations were checked algebraically and at finite controls. They have no new Lean or human validation. The next mathematical decision is whether the geometry of the actual recursive view permits smaller cost for a specified accepted-site defect or outer residual slack. The crude lower bound uses only maximum view overlap. The positive bound pays for a general-purpose small-bias sampler. A reduction to the resulting *Boolean* approximation threshold would also have to price the added clauses.
